# 1. BRAND FOUNDATION

## 1.1 Brand Name

**SAVORLEN**

SAVORLEN is the master consumer-facing brand. Product categories and individual products appear under this name.

Examples:

* SAVORLEN Fish Masala
* SAVORLEN Chicken Masala
* SAVORLEN Garam Masala
* SAVORLEN Spice Blends

The master brand must remain consistent across every product, platform, and marketing channel.

Do not append words such as "Foods," "Industries," or "Enterprises" to the consumer-facing logo unless a specific legal or administrative application requires the registered business name.

## 1.2 Brand Positioning

SAVORLEN is a premium food-ingredient brand built around flavour, quality, trust, and modern presentation.

The visual identity must communicate:

* **Premium:** Considered, polished, and professionally executed.
* **Flavour-focused:** Food should look appetising, rich, and authentic.
* **Trustworthy:** Clear information, honest communication, and consistent quality.
* **Natural:** Warm materials, believable ingredients, and an approachable character.
* **Modern:** Contemporary typography, structured layouts, and disciplined visual design.
* **Hygienic:** Clean presentation, legible packaging, and an organised visual hierarchy.
* **Scalable:** A unified identity that works for a single spice blend or a larger product portfolio.

SAVORLEN should feel like a professionally managed packaged-food brand rather than a traditional unbranded spice shop or a generic luxury fashion label.

## 1.3 Brand Personality

The brand is:

* Bold, but not aggressive.
* Premium, but not inaccessible.
* Modern, but not excessively futuristic.
* Natural, but not rustic or old-fashioned.
* Minimal, but not empty or sterile.
* Corporate, but not cold.
* Flavourful, but not visually chaotic.

## 1.4 Design Principles

Every design decision should follow these principles, in order:

1. Clarity before decoration.
2. Product recognition before visual effects.
3. Readability before stylistic experimentation.
4. Authenticity before artificial perfection.
5. Consistency before novelty.
6. Distinctiveness without sacrificing usability.
7. Premium execution without unnecessary expense.

# 2. OFFICIAL COLOUR SYSTEM

## 2.1 Core Palette

The following colours are the official SAVORLEN palette.

| Colour           | Hex       | Role                               |
| ---------------- | --------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Warm Porcelain   | `#FAF9F4` | Main light background              |
| Vanilla          | `#EEEBDA` | Brand surface and packaging colour |
| Midnight Indigo  | `#282B4A` | Primary brand colour and main text |
| Muted Terracotta | `#C47C65` | Secondary accent                   |

These values must remain consistent across websites, packaging, advertisements, documents, and digital content.

### A. Warm Porcelain — `#FAF9F4`

**Role:** Default background.

Use for:

* Website backgrounds.
* Product catalogues.
* Brand presentations.
* Corporate documents.
* Light promotional graphics.
* Editorial layouts.

This colour provides a soft, warm alternative to pure white.

### B. Vanilla — `#EEEBDA`

**Role:** Signature light surface.

Use for:

* Packaging backgrounds.
* Logo backgrounds.
* Product cards.
* Secondary panels.
* Brand stationery.
* Social media templates.

Vanilla introduces warmth without making the identity look rustic.

### C. Midnight Indigo — `#282B4A`

**Role:** Primary identity colour.

Use for:

* Main logo.
* Primary typography.
* Product names.
* Headings.
* Website navigation.
* Dark packaging.
* Brand signatures and important graphic elements.

Midnight Indigo should carry most of the brand's visual authority.

### D. Muted Terracotta — `#C47C65`

**Role:** Supporting accent.

Use for:

* Product-category indicators.
* Small graphic details.
* Limited promotional highlights.
* Secondary buttons and markers.
* Small packaging accents.
* Selected dividers and decorative details.

Terracotta must remain restrained. It should not overpower Midnight Indigo or become the dominant colour of the entire brand.

## 2.2 Colour Distribution

As a starting guideline for general brand graphics:

* Warm Porcelain: 45%
* Vanilla: 25%
* Midnight Indigo: 25%
* Muted Terracotta: 5%

These proportions are flexible, not mandatory.

Dark packaging may use Midnight Indigo as the dominant colour. Product photography may introduce additional colours naturally.

## 2.3 Approved Colour Combinations

**Combination A — Primary Brand Signature**

Midnight Indigo + Vanilla

Use for:

* Main logo.
* Product packaging.
* Brand stationery.
* Premium promotional graphics.

**Combination B — Corporate Minimalism**

Warm Porcelain + Midnight Indigo

Use for:

* Websites.
* Presentations.
* Product catalogues.
* Corporate communication.

**Combination C — Restrained Warmth**

Vanilla + Muted Terracotta + Midnight Indigo

Use for:

* Product highlights.
* Secondary packaging elements.
* Food-related promotional graphics.

Midnight Indigo should generally carry the text.

**Combination D — Dark Premium**

Midnight Indigo + Vanilla, with limited Terracotta accents.

Use for:

* Premium product launches.
* Dark packaging.
* Advertising campaigns.
* Brand announcement graphics.

## 2.4 Colour Restrictions

Do not introduce the following as permanent brand colours without deliberate approval:

* Bright red.
* Saturated yellow.
* Neon green.
* Electric blue.
* Purple gradients.
* Excessive metallic gold.
* Multiple competing accent colours.

Food photography can naturally contain these colours when appropriate.

Do not apply gradients simply to make a design look modern.

## 2.5 Contrast and Accessibility

* Use Midnight Indigo for text on Warm Porcelain or Vanilla wherever practical.
* Use Vanilla or Warm Porcelain for text on Midnight Indigo.
* Do not rely on Muted Terracotta for small text on light backgrounds.
* Do not communicate product categories using colour alone.
* Keep essential product information readable in print and on mobile screens.

For digital interfaces, check actual colour contrast before release. Aim for WCAG AA contrast for ordinary text wherever applicable.

# 3. TYPOGRAPHY SYSTEM

## 3.1 Primary Typeface: Michroma

**Official typeface:** Michroma
**Primary purpose:** Brand wordmark and selected display typography.

Michroma provides the identity with a geometric, technical, spacious, and futuristic appearance.

It should be used deliberately. Because it is a distinctive display face, using it for every paragraph would reduce readability and make the brand feel excessively technological.

Recommended source:

https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Michroma

## 3.2 Logo Typography

The official wordmark is:

SAVORLEN

Requirements:

* Uppercase lettering.
* Michroma as the starting typeface.
* Clean, geometric construction.
* Carefully controlled spacing.
* Strong horizontal presence.
* No italic styling.
* No script or calligraphic elements.
* No unnecessary outlines, shadows, bevels, or 3D effects.

The final logo may include carefully refined custom letterforms, provided the word remains immediately readable.

Do not distort the letters horizontally or vertically to create an artificially wider appearance.

Do not force excessive letter spacing if it makes the name difficult to read at smaller sizes.

## 3.3 Supporting Typeface: Inter

**Supporting typeface:** Inter
**Purpose:** Readability, information, and functional typography.

Recommended source:

https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Inter

Use Inter for:

* Website body text.
* Product descriptions.
* Ingredients.
* Instructions.
* Packaging information.
* Social media captions rendered inside graphics.
* Advertising details.
* Corporate documents.
* Product catalogues.
* Prices and product specifications.

Recommended weights:

| Weight   | Value | Application                        |
| -------- | ----: | ---------------------------------- |
| Regular  |   400 | Body text                          |
| Medium   |   500 | Supporting information             |
| SemiBold |   600 | Labels and subheadings             |
| Bold     |   700 | Important information and emphasis |

## 3.4 Typography Hierarchy

| Element                   | Typeface          | Recommended weight                      |
| ------------------------- | ----------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Master logo               | Michroma          | Customised wordmark                     |
| Campaign headline         | Michroma          | Regular, with size and spacing adjusted |
| Product name              | Michroma or Inter | Use Michroma selectively                |
| Website heading           | Inter             | Bold or SemiBold                        |
| Website body              | Inter             | Regular                                 |
| Product description       | Inter             | Regular                                 |
| Ingredient list           | Inter             | Regular                                 |
| Small labels              | Inter             | Medium or SemiBold                      |
| Prices and specifications | Inter             | SemiBold                                |
| Legal information         | Inter             | Regular, subject to print legibility    |

Michroma does not need synthetic bolding to become the brand's identity. Its native letterforms are part of the intended aesthetic.

For a heavy visual headline, use a large Michroma wordmark or pair Michroma with bold Inter. Do not artificially embolden the typeface in ways that damage its letterforms.

## 3.5 Typography Rules

* Use no more than two typeface families in standard brand materials.
* Use uppercase selectively for headlines and product names.
* Use sentence case for descriptions and explanatory content.
* Keep paragraph line lengths comfortable.
* Maintain sufficient spacing between headings and body text.
* Avoid excessive tracking in long headings.
* Avoid condensed typography for essential product information.
* Do not use decorative fonts merely to make a design feel premium.
* Do not set long paragraphs in Michroma.
* Never sacrifice readability for futuristic styling.

## 3.6 Product Naming Hierarchy

Example:

SAVORLEN

FISH MASALA

A flavourful blend for fish dishes.

The master brand should be recognisable first. The product name must be immediately visible, and the supporting description must remain secondary.

The exact product description must accurately reflect the product.

# 4. LOGO AND BRAND MARK SYSTEM

## 4.1 Primary Logo

The primary logo is a typography-led wordmark:

SAVORLEN

The wordmark should be distinctive enough to function independently without requiring an icon.

Its visual character should be:

* Bold.
* Geometric.
* Minimal.
* Spacious.
* Precise.
* Modern.
* Corporate.
* Premium.

The design should rely on the quality of the lettering rather than decorative effects.

## 4.2 Logo Variations

Develop these versions as part of the identity system:

**Version A — Primary Wordmark**

Midnight Indigo wordmark on Vanilla or Warm Porcelain.

**Version B — Reverse Wordmark**

Vanilla or Warm Porcelain wordmark on Midnight Indigo.

**Version C — Single-Colour Wordmark**

Solid black or solid white for monochrome printing and situations where the primary palette is unavailable.

**Version D — Compact Mark**

An optional S-based monogram or abstract symbol for small applications, such as profile images, stickers, or compact brand placements.

The compact mark must be developed from the approved logo identity. It must not be an unrelated decorative icon.

## 4.3 Optional Symbol

A symbol is optional, not mandatory.

If a symbol is developed, it should be:

* Simple.
* Recognisable.
* Reproducible at small sizes.
* Compatible with the wordmark.
* Distinctive without being complicated.

Explore abstract geometry or a carefully customised letterform before considering a literal illustration.

Do not automatically use:

* Leaves.
* Chef hats.
* Cooking pots.
* Spoons.
* Forks.
* Flames.
* Generic spice jars.
* Stock botanical icons.

These elements are not forbidden when genuinely appropriate, but they must not be used as default shortcuts to communicate food.

## 4.4 Logo Construction and Spacing

* Preserve the original proportions of the approved logo.
* Maintain clear space around the wordmark.
* Never stretch, compress, rotate, or skew the logo.
* Do not place the logo over visually busy photography without sufficient separation.
* Do not add unapproved taglines inside the logo.
* Do not place shadows or outlines around the wordmark.
* Do not substitute random fonts for the approved logo artwork.

As an initial clear-space rule, reserve at least the height of the wordmark's capital letter around the logo. Refine this measurement when the final logo artwork is approved.

## 4.5 Logo Backgrounds

Preferred backgrounds:

1. Vanilla.
2. Warm Porcelain.
3. Midnight Indigo.

Terracotta can be used as a secondary background only when contrast and brand consistency remain strong.

## 4.6 Logo Scalability

The logo must work:

* On small product labels.
* On large posters.
* On packaging.
* On websites.
* On social media profile images.
* In monochrome.
* In low-cost printing.
* At large outdoor advertising sizes.

Test the final logo at small sizes before approving it.

# 5. OVERALL VISUAL LANGUAGE

## 5.1 Core Aesthetic

SAVORLEN combines:

**Modern precision + natural warmth + premium food presentation.**

The visual language should feel organised, tactile, confident, and contemporary.

Use:

* Strong typography.
* Clear grids.
* Intentional negative space.
* Balanced layouts.
* Controlled colour blocks.
* Subtle geometric details.
* Authentic food photography.
* Consistent alignment.
* High-quality image cropping.

Avoid designs that look like generic AI-generated luxury branding.

## 5.2 Shapes and Geometry

Preferred shapes:

* Rectangles.
* Clean panels.
* Simple dividers.
* Restrained rounded corners.
* Structured grids.
* Carefully proportioned geometric forms.

Use shapes to organise information, not to decorate every empty space.

Do not use excessive rounded cards, floating circles, random blobs, or ornamental lines.

## 5.3 Lines and Borders

* Use thin, clean dividers where useful.
* Prefer consistent line weights.
* Avoid multiple decorative border systems.
* Avoid ornamental frames around every element.
* Keep borders secondary to the content.

## 5.4 Textures

Possible restrained textures:

* Subtle paper grain.
* Natural packaging material.
* Soft, realistic surface textures.
* Authentic ingredient textures in photography.

Textures must not interfere with legibility.

Avoid:

* Heavy grunge.
* Artificially distressed lettering.
* Fake vintage paper.
* Excessive noise.
* Unnecessary metallic textures.
* Synthetic plastic-looking surfaces.

## 5.5 Shadows and Effects

The default design system is flat and precise.

Avoid:

* Heavy drop shadows.
* Neon glows.
* Bevels.
* Embossed-looking digital logos.
* Metallic 3D text.
* Glassmorphism.
* Excessive gradients.
* Artificial lens flares.

Realistic lighting and physical shadows in product photography are acceptable.

# 6. FOOD PHOTOGRAPHY AND IMAGE DIRECTION

## 6.1 Photography Objective

Food imagery should make the product look appetising, authentic, and trustworthy.

The image must support the actual product rather than overwhelm the brand.

## 6.2 Photography Style

Preferred:

* Realistic food photography.
* Natural-looking ingredients.
* Controlled studio lighting.
* Clear subject separation.
* Rich but believable colours.
* Tactile textures.
* Carefully styled dishes.
* Clean backgrounds.
* Professional composition.

Avoid:

* Oversaturated food.
* Artificially glowing spices.
* Unrealistic ingredient arrangements.
* Excessive steam effects.
* Plastic-looking food.
* Unrelated garnishes.
* Overly elaborate restaurant plating when the product is an everyday cooking ingredient.

## 6.3 Lighting

Use soft directional light or carefully controlled studio lighting.

Highlights should reveal texture without making ingredients look oily, wet, or artificial.

Shadows should add depth without hiding the subject.

## 6.4 Backgrounds

Preferred photography backgrounds:

* Warm Porcelain.
* Vanilla.
* Natural neutral surfaces.
* Midnight Indigo for selected dark campaigns.
* Realistic kitchen or food-preparation environments.

Natural wood and stone can appear in photography, but should not become mandatory decorative motifs.

## 6.5 Ingredient Photography

When showing spices or ingredients:

* Use ingredients relevant to the actual product.
* Avoid inventing ingredients.
* Avoid presenting decorative ingredients as if they are necessarily included in the blend.
* Do not imply health benefits without reliable substantiation.
* Keep the product itself identifiable.

## 6.6 Image Authenticity

Never present an illustrative image as a photograph of the actual packaged product.

When using AI-generated food imagery:

* Review it for visual errors.
* Check ingredient plausibility.
* Inspect hands, utensils, containers, and food textures.
* Avoid fabricated packaging text.
* Add the actual approved logo and product information separately.
* Disclose illustrative imagery where required or where omission could mislead consumers.

Product photography should accurately represent the product being sold.

# 7. PACKAGING DESIGN SYSTEM

## 7.1 Packaging Objective

Packaging must communicate the brand, product identity, and essential product information quickly.

It should feel premium and modern without making production unnecessarily expensive.

## 7.2 Front-of-Pack Hierarchy

Use this hierarchy as the default:

1. SAVORLEN wordmark.
2. Product name.
3. Short, accurate product descriptor.
4. Product-specific visual or ingredient image, if appropriate.
5. Net quantity.
6. Other required front-of-pack information.

The precise hierarchy must be adapted to applicable food-labelling requirements.

The product name must remain highly visible. Do not allow the master logo to occupy so much space that the product becomes difficult to identify.

## 7.3 Packaging Colour Variations

Use the master palette consistently.

Possible applications:

**Light packaging**

* Vanilla or Warm Porcelain background.
* Midnight Indigo logo and typography.
* Small Terracotta accent.

**Dark premium packaging**

* Midnight Indigo background.
* Vanilla typography.
* Limited Terracotta details.

Product categories may receive restrained visual differentiation, but the identity should remain recognisably SAVORLEN.

Do not introduce a completely unrelated colour scheme for each product.

## 7.4 Material Direction

Potential options:

* Food-grade pouches.
* Printed flexible packaging.
* Labels on suitable food-grade containers.
* Cartons or secondary packaging where appropriate.

Choose materials based on:

* Food safety.
* Product compatibility.
* Moisture and oxygen barrier requirements.
* Shelf-life requirements.
* Cost.
* Printing quality.
* Supplier availability.

The appearance of a package must never take priority over food safety.

Use only materials suitable for their intended food-contact application.

## 7.5 Finishes

Possible premium finishes, depending on cost:

* Matte finish.
* Selective gloss.
* Subtle foil accents.
* Embossing.

These are optional.

A well-designed standard matte package is preferable to an expensive package with weak typography or poor information hierarchy.

## 7.6 Back-of-Pack Information

Include all information required for the product and applicable jurisdiction.

Depending on the product, this may include:

* Legal product name.
* Ingredient list.
* Allergen declaration.
* Net quantity.
* Nutritional information where required.
* Manufacturer, packer, or marketer details as applicable.
* FSSAI-related details where required.
* Batch or lot identification.
* Date marking.
* Storage instructions.
* Usage instructions.
* MRP and consumer-care information as applicable.
* Barcode where required by the chosen sales channel.

Do not invent certifications, licence numbers, nutritional claims, manufacturing details, or shelf-life statements.

Final packaging must be reviewed for current Indian food-labelling and legal-metrology requirements before printing.

## 7.7 Packaging Readability

* Use readable type sizes.
* Maintain strong contrast.
* Avoid placing small text over photographs.
* Keep critical information away from folds and seals.
* Confirm that text remains legible on the actual printed material.
* Test the packaging at its real physical size.

Do not rely on a large digital mockup to judge small printed text.

# 8. WEBSITE DESIGN SYSTEM

## 8.1 Website Objective

The website should make SAVORLEN appear credible, premium, easy to understand, and easy to navigate.

It should communicate:

* What the brand sells.
* What makes the products distinctive.
* Which products are available.
* How the products are used.
* How customers can contact or purchase from the business.

## 8.2 Website Colour Usage

Default light theme:

* Page background: Warm Porcelain.
* Product and content surfaces: Vanilla.
* Main typography: Midnight Indigo.
* Primary buttons: Midnight Indigo.
* Primary button text: Vanilla or another verified high-contrast light colour.
* Secondary highlights: Muted Terracotta.

Use dark sections selectively rather than making every page dark.

## 8.3 Layout

* Use a clear content grid.
* Maintain consistent horizontal alignment.
* Provide comfortable spacing.
* Keep navigation simple.
* Use strong product imagery.
* Establish clear heading hierarchy.
* Avoid excessive cards and decorative components.
* Design for mobile devices first, then adapt for larger screens.

## 8.4 Buttons

Primary buttons:

* Midnight Indigo background.
* Light text.
* Clear action labels.
* Simple rectangular or subtly rounded shape.

Secondary buttons:

* Transparent or light background.
* Midnight Indigo text.
* Restrained border where appropriate.

Terracotta may be used for selected accents but should not be used indiscriminately for every button.

## 8.5 Components

Reusable components should include:

* Header and navigation.
* Footer.
* Product cards.
* Product detail sections.
* Ingredient and usage information.
* Brand story sections.
* Contact details.
* Notices and alerts.
* Buttons and links.
* Forms, if needed.
* Promotional banners.

Each component must use the same typography, spacing, colour, and interaction rules.

## 8.6 Interaction and Animation

Animations should be subtle and functional.

Appropriate examples:

* Small hover transitions.
* Gentle opacity transitions.
* Simple section reveals.
* Clear button feedback.

Avoid:

* Excessive parallax.
* Constantly moving elements.
* Distracting scroll effects.
* Long loading animations.
* Animations that prevent access to content.
* Effects that make the website feel like a technology demo.

Respect reduced-motion preferences.

## 8.7 Accessibility and Performance

* Use semantic HTML.
* Maintain keyboard accessibility.
* Provide visible focus states.
* Use meaningful alternative text.
* Maintain readable contrast.
* Optimise image sizes.
* Avoid unnecessary scripts.
* Keep the website responsive.
* Ensure essential information is available without animation.
* Test the website on low-end mobile devices and slower connections.

# 9. ADVERTISEMENTS AND CAMPAIGN DESIGN

## 9.1 Campaign Objective

Every advertisement should communicate one primary message.

Examples:

* Introduce a new product.
* Highlight a specific flavour profile.
* Demonstrate a cooking application.
* Announce product availability.
* Promote an offer.
* Build brand awareness.

Do not attempt to communicate every brand benefit in a single advertisement.

## 9.2 Recommended Ad Structure

1. Brand identifier.
2. Main headline.
3. Product or food visual.
4. One supporting message.
5. Call to action, where appropriate.
6. Relevant legal or promotional information.

The product should be identifiable quickly.

## 9.3 Headline Style

Use concise, confident language.

Preferred:

* Clear product-led headlines.
* Short flavour-focused statements.
* Direct product announcements.
* Simple, memorable campaign concepts.

Avoid:

* Excessive punctuation.
* Overly aggressive sales language.
* Unverifiable superlatives.
* Artificial urgency.
* Long paragraphs inside graphics.
* Generic slogans that do not communicate a meaningful benefit.

## 9.4 Visual Style

Use:

* Large typography.
* Controlled colour blocks.
* Strong product photography.
* Consistent logo placement.
* Clear spacing.
* Minimal supporting elements.

Avoid:

* Multiple competing headlines.
* Too many badges.
* Unrelated decorative ingredients.
* Fake review stars.
* Fabricated awards.
* Excessive sale stickers.
* Unnecessary 3D text.

## 9.5 Advertising Claims

Only publish claims that can be supported.

Do not claim:

* "Best in India" without adequate substantiation.
* "100% pure" without ensuring the claim is accurate and compliant.
* "Chemical-free" as an unsupported absolute claim.
* "Organic" without meeting applicable requirements.
* Medical or disease-related benefits without appropriate evidence and legal clearance.
* Artificially low prices or false discounts.

Promotional information must match the actual offer.

# 10. SOCIAL MEDIA DESIGN SYSTEM

## 10.1 General Rule

Social media content should be recognisable as SAVORLEN even when the logo is not the largest element.

Consistency should come from:

* Typography.
* Colour palette.
* Image treatment.
* Layout.
* Logo placement.
* Tone of communication.

Do not make every post identical. Maintain the system while adapting the composition to the content.

## 10.2 Content Categories

Create reusable visual templates for:

**Product Spotlight**

* Product name.
* Product image.
* One concise supporting message.

**Recipe or Cooking Inspiration**

* Food photograph.
* Recipe or dish name.
* Relevant product association.

**Product Education**

* Clear headline.
* Useful factual information.
* Supporting visual.

**Brand Story**

* Concise message.
* Appropriate photography.
* Restrained brand styling.

**Launch Announcement**

* Product name.
* Strong product visual.
* Availability information.

**Offer or Promotion**

* Actual offer.
* Clear terms.
* Appropriate validity dates.

**Customer Feedback**

* Authentic feedback used with appropriate permission.
* No fabricated testimonials.

## 10.3 Layout Rules

* Use consistent margins.
* Keep text readable on mobile screens.
* Give the main headline sufficient space.
* Use high-quality photography.
* Avoid overcrowding.
* Keep decorative details secondary.
* Ensure the product is immediately recognisable.
* Avoid placing critical text near platform interface overlays.

## 10.4 Profile Images and Cover Graphics

Use the approved logo or approved compact mark.

For small profile images:

* Prefer simple shapes and strong contrast.
* Avoid the full wordmark if it becomes unreadable at the displayed size.
* Do not create a different logo for each social platform.

# 11. POSTERS, BANNERS, AND PRINT MATERIALS

## 11.1 Posters

Posters must communicate their main message from a distance.

Use:

* One primary headline.
* One dominant visual.
* A clear brand identifier.
* Minimal supporting text.
* Strong contrast.
* A visible call to action when needed.

Do not fill the entire poster with product images, text, and decorative shapes.

## 11.2 Banners

For banners:

* Prioritise the brand and key message.
* Use larger text than on ordinary digital graphics.
* Avoid small supporting information.
* Account for viewing distance.
* Ensure critical elements are not cropped in production.

## 11.3 Business Cards and Stationery

Use:

* Vanilla or Warm Porcelain backgrounds.
* Midnight Indigo typography.
* A restrained Terracotta detail, if useful.
* Clean alignment.
* Consistent logo proportions.

Do not use unnecessary icons, ornamental borders, or decorative background patterns.

## 11.4 Print Production

Before printing:

* Confirm the final dimensions.
* Use the printer's required colour profile and file specifications.
* Convert colours appropriately for the production process.
* Embed or outline fonts where necessary.
* Include bleed and safe margins.
* Check image resolution.
* Inspect a physical proof for packaging and important printed materials.

HEX values are primarily for digital use. Printed colour will vary with substrate, ink, and production settings. Approve print colours using physical proofs when colour accuracy matters.

## 12.1 Catalogues

A product catalogue should use:

* Consistent product photography.
* Uniform image treatment.
* Standardised product-name placement.
* Clear product descriptions.
* Consistent specifications.
* Restrained brand colours.

Products must be easy to compare.

## 12.2 Presentations

Use:

* Strong headings.
* Short, clear content.
* Consistent alignment.
* Restrained colour.
* Useful imagery.
* Clear data visualisation.

Avoid overdecorating charts or using colour without a meaningful purpose.

## 12.3 Documents

Use Inter as the default document font.

Use Midnight Indigo for headings and important labels.

Use Warm Porcelain or white backgrounds for long-form documents to preserve readability and reduce printing costs.

# 13. PRODUCT CATEGORY DIFFERENTIATION

## 13.1 Master Brand Consistency

Every product must clearly belong to SAVORLEN.

Maintain:

* The same master logo.
* The same core typography.
* The same fundamental colour identity.
* The same information hierarchy.
* The same general quality standard.

## 13.2 Product Differentiation

Different products may use:

* Different food photography.
* Different product names.
* Distinct but restrained category markers.
* Different compositions within the same layout system.
* Product-specific descriptions.

Do not redesign the entire identity for every new product.

Do not assign a new permanent colour palette to every product without a strategic reason.

## 13.3 Product Accuracy

Product names, ingredients, photographs, and descriptions must correspond to the actual product.

Do not use chicken imagery for a fish product or suggest that a blend contains ingredients that are not present.

Where dietary, allergen, or vegetarian/non-vegetarian indicators are applicable, follow current legal requirements and ensure the indicators are accurate.

## 14.1 Tone of Voice

SAVORLEN communicates in a way that is:

* Clear.
* Confident.
* Warm.
* Professional.
* Taste-focused.
* Trustworthy.
* Concise.

Avoid:

* Exaggerated luxury language.
* Excessive technical jargon.
* Unsupported health claims.
* Forced slang.
* Overly regional expressions in the core identity.
* Generic AI-sounding marketing copy.

The brand may acknowledge its origins and production story where authentic, but its master identity should remain accessible to customers across India and potentially international markets.

## 14.2 Tagline

No tagline is officially approved in this document.

A proposed tagline must be reviewed separately for meaning, originality, readability, and commercial suitability before becoming part of the permanent identity.

Do not invent a tagline and treat it as an established brand asset.

## 14.3 Language and Localisation

The primary brand identity uses the English spelling SAVORLEN.

Supporting content may use additional languages when appropriate for the target market.

Translations must be accurate and culturally appropriate. Do not alter the master logo to create language-specific variations unless a formal localisation system is approved.

# 16. AI DESIGN AND IMAGE-GENERATION RULES

These instructions apply when using AI tools to create brand assets.

## 16.1 Preserve Brand Consistency

Every generated design must follow:

* The approved colour palette.
* The typography hierarchy.
* The visual language.
* The product information hierarchy.
* The logo usage rules.
* The appropriate output format.

## 16.2 Prevent Generic AI Aesthetics

Do not default to:

* Purple-blue gradients.
* Glowing outlines.
* Floating 3D objects.
* Random geometric blobs.
* Unnecessary glassmorphism.
* Overly polished synthetic food.
* Generic gold luxury effects.
* Decorative leaves added without purpose.
* Excessive icons.
* Unrelated visual motifs.

Every visual element must have a clear reason to exist.

## 16.3 Logo Generation

When generating a logo concept:

* Use SAVORLEN exactly as spelled.
* Use Michroma as the typographic reference.
* Prioritise a clean wordmark.
* Preserve legibility.
* Use Midnight Indigo and Vanilla as the primary combination.
* Avoid unnecessary symbols.
* Avoid gradients and 3D effects.
* Avoid mockup backgrounds when a clean logo asset is required.

AI-generated lettering may contain errors. The final wordmark should be checked and, where necessary, rebuilt as proper vector artwork.

## 16.4 Text Inside Generated Images

Do not rely on image-generation tools for final packaging text, legal information, prices, or detailed advertisements.

Generate the visual composition first, then place accurate text and the approved logo using a design tool.

Verify every word before publication.

## 16.5 Product Representation

Never fabricate:

* Product packaging details.
* Ingredient lists.
* Certifications.
* Customer reviews.
* Manufacturing information.
* Nutritional claims.
* Awards.
* Prices.
* Availability.

Use approved product information as the source of truth.

# 17. ACCESSIBILITY, LEGIBILITY, AND QUALITY CONTROL

Every design must be checked for:

* Readable typography.
* Sufficient contrast.
* Correct spelling.
* Correct logo.
* Correct colour values.
* Consistent alignment.
* Proper margins.
* Accurate product details.
* Appropriate image quality.
* Platform-specific cropping.
* Print suitability where relevant.

For digital work:

* Test on mobile and desktop.
* Check contrast.
* Ensure important text is not too small.
* Verify that the logo remains recognisable.

For physical packaging:

* Review the actual-size artwork.
* Inspect a physical proof.
* Confirm legal and product information.
* Check the material and printing process.

For advertisements:

* Verify every claim.
* Verify offer details.
* Confirm the call to action.
* Check that the product image represents the real product.

A visually attractive design is not approved until its content is accurate and usable.

# 18. WHAT SAVORLEN MUST NEVER LOOK LIKE

Unless a specific campaign deliberately requires a deviation, avoid designs that resemble:

* A generic local spice shop.
* A cluttered traditional masala advertisement.
* A discount-only retail brand.
* An unrelated technology startup.
* A futuristic gaming company.
* An imitation luxury perfume brand.
* A rustic organic brand built entirely around leaves and kraft paper.
* A generic AI-generated logo.
* A colourful children's food brand.
* A corporate brand with no warmth or food appeal.

SAVORLEN should balance technical precision with the warmth and authenticity expected from food products.

# 19. DESIGN DECISION FRAMEWORK

Before creating any asset, answer these questions:

1. What is the purpose of this asset?
2. Who is the intended audience?
3. What is the one most important message?
4. Which product or brand element must receive attention?
5. Which approved colours are most appropriate?
6. Is Michroma suitable for the headline, or would Inter be more readable?
7. Does the imagery accurately represent the product?
8. Is every claim supportable?
9. Is the design readable at its actual display or print size?
10. Does it look recognisably like SAVORLEN?
11. Is every decorative element necessary?
12. Does the final output meet the technical requirements of its intended use?

If a design fails any critical check, revise it before release.
